# tools/notice-when-something-shows-up/

A resident process that reads standing orders (waiter rows), starts the waiting mechanism each one names, and appends one self-describing fact each time something outside became reachable or stopped being reachable: time passing, a storage volume attached or removed, a delivery arriving over the network, a unit of files somebody finished putting in a place. It never dispatches anything; whatever wants to react reads the facts. The clock is one waiter row whose projection reproduces the old clock tick record byte for byte.

Moved byte for byte from the old `notice-when-something-shows-up` package of `bitfield-packages` (`moved-from.json` is the receipt).

## Time as a fact (T4.3)

When something is due belongs to timed-action alone (`tools/run-work-later-or-on-a-repeat`): its schedules by minute, hour, day or week in any IANA zone, each with its own clock-change rule, its one-off actions, its occurrence keys and its spread. The due-time leaf only asks it (`whenToWake`), waits until the time it names, and tells what came due. It holds no time arithmetic of its own.

- **Exactly once is the engine's.** The engine admits one fact per teller, kind and event id, and its marker lasts (trillion-rust src/wake.rs `fact`). Each occurrence is told under timed-action's occurrence key, so telling it again after a restart starts nothing.
- **The fact is timed-action's owner proof.** An occurrence counts as done only once the engine took its fact; a refused tell leaves it due, and it is told again after `retry_seconds`.
- **Its memory is the row's kept folder** (`invoke.kept_folder`, already in the engine, trillion-rust src/tool_runs/tools/container.rs `kept_folder`). Every mechanism gets its waiter's kept state (`state`), a way to keep more (`keepState`) and a folder of its own in the kept folder (`folder`, for files such as a linked device's session; null without a kept folder). A waiter that reads with no credential may name the place its facts are told as in its row (`where`).
- **The state stays bounded.** timed-action gained `resume_from_ms`: occurrences starting before it count as passed. Per schedule the leaf keeps only the proof of its last told occurrence and moves `resume_from_ms` to its start, so the state is the same size in year ten as in week one. Set at the waiter's first run, it also makes a new schedule start from now instead of catching up since `start_local_date`. Any other caller of timed-action can keep its state bounded the same way.
- **What a sleep does is timed-action's rule, not the leaf's:** any schedule asleep through many times runs once on waking, for its latest time, and its fact's `missed_before` says how many it passed over.
- **A wake asks for its own schedule:** `{fact: {kind: due-time, from: [run], match: {schedule_id}, watch: {schedules: [<timed-action schedule>]}}}`. The engine never reads `watch`; it keeps and lists it (`wake:list`). The noticer's waiter (`from_wakes: {kind: due-time}`) reads the switched-on wakes that hear due-time, about once a minute (`look_again_seconds`), and watches what they ask. So each schedule lives once, on the row that wants it, and only schedules of wakes the person switched on are watched. Its row's right for due-time names both `wake:arrive` and `wake:list`.
- **The budget stays the person's work.** A fact a run tells draws from the work ledger (trillion-rust src/wake/wake.json `draws.facts`), as the time wakes it replaces did; what the computer or a sender tells stays within the inbound ledger.
